PROTOTYPE: the Living War as a true simulation

Throwaway (wayfinder "Prototype a headless army simulation against the pacing targets"). Six real worlds exported from the game: their biomes, roads, seats, houses, lords and wars. The armies follow the decisions already made: they muster from house strength, march along roads (the maze is closed to them), choose objectives by their captain's traits, fight one battle state whether watched or not, and take seats. Nothing is ever placed near the player.
The question: does the honest war still come near a travelling player often enough? The targets are a visible encounter every 80 to 120 overland turns, about one pitched battle per expedition, and wars that end in 8 to 16 Seasons with both houses still standing.
Grey: open land. Dark blue: sea. Brown: mountains. Purple: the central maze. Gold lines: roads. Squares: seats in house colours. Circles: armies (size grows with soldiers; a ring marks a levy, a small dot a raiding party). Red X: a battle under way. White: the player.
