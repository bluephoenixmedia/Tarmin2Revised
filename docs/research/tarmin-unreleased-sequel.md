# The unreleased sequel to Treasure of Tarmin

Research for #275, part of the map #265 (the World Log and the myth of the Eternal Champion).
The user's question: a sequel to Mattel's *Advanced Dungeons & Dragons: Treasure of Tarmin*
(Intellivision, 1983) was planned and never released, and the **Ghost** and **Old Dwarf**
statue shrines come from its hidden lore (#272, Q7). What is documented about it?

## Answer in brief

- **There were two unfinished follow-ups to Treasure of Tarmin, and neither has any lore.**
  1. **Tom Loughry's own third AD&D game (1982).** Loughry wrote Treasure of Tarmin. He and another APh
     programmer started a third AD&D game, which was abandoned when he left APh [WL, CA]. It has no title,
     premise, story or creatures on record. The only description is that it would have added saving, more
     complex combat and overland exploration, "kind of like *Zelda*" [CA]. All of this comes second-hand from
     a 2014 podcast interview [P6].
  2. **Mattel's "D&D III" (1983).** Its working titles were *Arcade D&D*, then *AD&D Revenge of the Master*,
     then *AD&D Tower of Mystery*. Daniel Bass designed it as an action-heavy follow-up because Marketing
     found Tarmin "a bit too complex". It was half done when Mattel Electronics closed in January 1984. INTV
     Corp. had it finished and released it in early 1987 as ***Tower of Doom***, without the D&D licence
     [BSR-T, IL-T, DP]. It **was released**, so it is not a lost game. It is a different game with a
     different setting: a tower you escape downward, not the island of Tarmin.
- **Neither project has any documented ghost, spirit, elder or old dwarf.** No source mentions Tarmin, the
  Minotaur or the island in connection with either one.
- **Conclusion:** no reliable source supports a "hidden lore" for the Ghost and Old Dwarf. Those two gods
  remain this game's inventions, and naming them is the user's decision (#272).

## 1. Tom Loughry's abandoned third AD&D game

- Treasure of Tarmin was written by **Tom Loughry** at APh Technology Consultants, in production from 1981.
  Its working titles were *Minotaur* and *D&D II* [BSR-A]. APh also planned an enhanced Keyboard Component
  version "featuring synchronized voices", which "was never started" [BSR-A]. That is a version of
  Tarmin, not a sequel.
- **The third game.** "In a 2014 interview, APh programmer Tom Loughry explains that in 1982 he and another
  APh programmer started a third Advanced Dungeons & Dragons game which was abandoned when he left the
  company" [WL, citing P6]. Loughry left APh in 1982 to co-found Cheshire Engineering [SM, biographical note].
- **What it would have been.** Per [CA], relaying the same interview, Loughry was "disappointed with features
  that he couldn't offer, including saving, more complex combat, and an overland exploration ability. He
  planned to offer such features in a third *Advanced Dungeons & Dragons* title, but the project died during
  development. (He says it would have been 'kind of like *Zelda*.')"
- **Not documented:** a title, a story, a setting, whether it continued Tarmin's world, or any monster.
  Nothing places a ghost or an old dwarf in it.
- **Possible surviving material, unexamined.** The Strong holds Loughry's papers, 7 boxes, bulk 1980-1985
  [SM]. They include "proposals, unused designs" and:
  - "Unused design proposals, 1981-1982" (box 1 folder 11);
  - "Game proposals, 1981 June" (box 1 folder 7);
  - source code for "Advanced D&D, 1982" (box 1 folder 12), "Minotaur, 1981" and "Minotaur development
    documents and source code, 1981" (box 3 folders 4-5);
  - an unexplained item, "Krozar", 1981 March (box 3 folder 3).

  The finding aid does not say whether any of these is the third game. "Advanced D&D, 1982" may simply be
  Cloudy Mountain, the 1982 *Advanced Dungeons & Dragons* cartridge. The source code is on-site only [SM].
  **This archive is the one place a design document for Tarmin's real successor could exist.**

## 2. "D&D III": Revenge of the Master, then Tower of Mystery, then Tower of Doom

- **Origin.** "With the success of the first *Dungeons & Dragons* cartridge, Marketing wanted a continuing
  series of D&D games. They were a bit concerned that the second release, [...] TREASURE OF TARMIN [...], was a
  bit too complex, so when adding a third D&D game to the schedule, they took to calling it *Arcade D&D*.
  'Arcade' was their code word for more action, less brains" [BSR-T; IL-T].
- **Names.** Mattel catalog #4692. AKA *D&D III*, *Arcade D&D*, and *AD&D Revenge of the Master*, the name
  of "a limited demo [...] at one trade show". It was then renamed *AD&D Tower of Mystery* for the catalogs
  [BSR-T]. No source says who "the Master" was.
- **People.**
  - Intellivision design and program: **Daniel (Dan) Bass**, finished by **John Tomlinson**.
  - Graphics: Monique Lujan-Bakerink, Connie Goldman, Karl Morris.
  - Music and sound: David Warhol, Joshua Jeffe [BSR-T].
  - The game was finished by Realtime Associates for INTV Corp. [BSR-T].
- **How far it got.**
  - Bass: "I only had the game about 80% done when Mattel Electronics went out of business. I had
    concentrated on the special effects and mechanics, but I hadn't put in the game play and strategy that I
    had had in mind." The finisher "didn't add any gameplay either, he just tidied up the loose ends so that
    the game had an ending and wouldn't crash" [DP].
  - Bass started it in summer 1983, and it "was originally supposed to be a voice-optional game" [DP].
  - The Blue Sky Rangers call it "only half-completed" in January 1984 [BSR-T].
- **Release.** "Not wanting to pay for the Dungeons & Dragons license, INTV released the cartridge in early
  1987 under the new name, *Tower of Doom*" (INTV #8600) [BSR-T]. The Atari 2600 and Apple versions had
  "little or no work" [BSR-T]. A separate 2600 *Tower of Mystery*, by Jane Terjung and Mike Sanders, was
  about a month from completion at the closing. Only the January 1984 CES demo survives [BSR-M].
- **Premise (the 1984 CES catalog).** "Stuck on top of the Tower of Mystery, you must preserve your strength
  and protect the treasure as you attempt to escape. Find treasure in the mazes of the tower!" [BSR-T; BSR-M].
- **Premise (the 1987 game).** You begin at the top of the Tower of Doom and descend through maze levels "to
  freedom". There are 10 characters (Novice, Warrior, Archer, Knight, Trader, Barbarian, Waif, Friar,
  Warlord, Warlock) and seven adventures. The hardest, Grail Quest, requires finding the Grail. Monsters
  are fought, bribed or fled [MAN]. **The manual names no monster at all**; the INTV catalog shows
  only "A dragon blocks the way!" [BSR-T].
- **Creatures (the 2600 *Tower of Mystery* only, fan source).** [AP] lists beholder, wraith ("a ghostly
  apparition"), serpent, bat, mummy, and an "evil lich Aga Aga". Its three win conditions are escaping,
  rescuing a kidnapped princess, or killing Aga Aga. These details are **not** in the Blue Sky Rangers
  pages or the Intellivision manual, so treat them as unverified for the 2600 demo only. [AP] also reports
  that two copies of the 2600 prototype surfaced in 2024, which differs from [BSR-M]'s "all that survives is
  the demo".
- **No tie to Tarmin's world.** No source mentions the island, the Minotaur, the Treasure, the War/Spiritual
  split or any Tarmin creature carrying over. The only link is the D&D licence series.

## 3. The Ghost and the Old Dwarf

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| A sequel to Treasure of Tarmin was planned and never released | **Documented, with a qualification.** Loughry's own third AD&D game was started in 1982 and abandoned [WL/P6, CA]. Mattel's "D&D III" also went unfinished at Mattel, but INTV released it in 1987 as *Tower of Doom* [BSR-T, DP]. |
| The sequel had a ghost or spirit | **Not supported.** Loughry's game has no recorded creatures. *Tower of Doom*'s manual names none. Only a fan page lists a "wraith (a ghostly apparition)" in the 2600 *Tower of Mystery* [AP], and Treasure of Tarmin already had Wraiths [OT]. |
| The sequel had an elder or old dwarf | **Not found in any source.** Treasure of Tarmin's own Dwarfs and Dwarfs with shields are the only dwarfs on record [OT]. |
| The Ghost and Old Dwarf come from a sequel's "hidden lore" | **No evidence.** No source describes any lore for either sequel beyond the CES catalog's single sentence. |

What this offers the gods, honestly:

- **The Old Dwarf** can descend from Treasure of Tarmin's own **Dwarfs** (Bad monsters, beaten only by
  spiritual weapons [OT]). The new game supplies the age.
- **The Ghost** can descend from Treasure of Tarmin's **Wraiths** (Horrible monsters [OT]), or from the 2600
  *Tower of Mystery*'s wraith if a weak fan source is acceptable.
- **The "lost sequel" can stay as in-fiction framing,** with a real-world kernel. Tarmin's author did plan a
  bigger successor with an overland world, and it died unmade. That fits #265's banished and forgotten old
  creatures (#272, Q3). The game must not present it as historical fact that the lost sequel contained
  these gods.
- **"Revenge of the Master"** is a real, unexplained working title from the sequel lineage [BSR-T]. It is a
  free hook (the old order's revenge) with no canon behind it.

## Caveats

- **[P6] was not heard.** Every claim about Loughry's third game comes through two secondary write-ups of
  one interview. [CA] says the podcast was "just this past June", while [WL] dates Episode 6 to February
  2014. Listening to Episode 6 at about 2:46h would settle what Loughry said, and might name the game.
- **The Strong archive is uncatalogued at item level.** Folder titles are all that is public. On-site
  research could find the third game's design.
- **The Blue Sky Rangers pages** are the developers' own retrospective histories, written decades later.
  They are the best available but are not contemporaneous documents.
- **AtariAge forum threads** about the 2600 prototypes returned 403 and were not read. No AtariAge material
  is relied on here.
- **Not found anywhere:** a "Treasure of Tarmin II", or any sequel tied to the island, the Minotaur, a ghost
  or an old dwarf. Searches covered Wikipedia, Blue Sky Rangers, Intellivision Lives archives, Digital
  Press, AtariProtos, The Strong and general web.
