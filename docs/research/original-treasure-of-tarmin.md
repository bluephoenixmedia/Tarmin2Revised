# The original Treasure of Tarmin (Mattel, Intellivision, 1983)

Research for #271, part of the map #265 (the World Log and the myth of the Eternal Champion).
Question: what did *Advanced Dungeons & Dragons: Treasure of Tarmin* establish about the
Minotaur, the treasure, the creatures and the world, so that the old gods of the new game
(the 13 statue shrines) have a faithful root?

## The premise: the whole story text

The manual's only narrative is its opening paragraph and "Object of the Game" [M, inside cover and p. 1 facing]:

> The DREADED MINOTAUR and his monster companions built a complicated system of MAZES and DUNGEONS in the island of Tarmin to hide and protect the Great Treasure of dark ages. Along with other stolen riches, they took OBJECTS of SPIRITUAL POWERS and LETHAL WEAPONS. Now the castle with its GHASTLY OCCUPANTS and FIERCELY GUARDED TREASURES has lured you in...You begin your JOURNEY wondering whether you'll ever make it to the end...

> ALONE in a deep web of endless rooms and corridors...you move on with a bow, a quiver of arrows, a sack of flour, and an empty pack toward DEEPER PATHWAYS...beyond which the TARMIN TREASURE can be found. Yet you know the DEVOURING MINOTAUR awaits you...at the end of your quest. [...] So it's up to you to OUTSMART the EVIL FORCES...and GRAB the LOOT!

Mattel's catalog description [IL]:

> You've found the secret map to the underground lair of the dreaded Minotaur. You can go in, but you'll never come out unless you slay the Minotaur and claim his Tarmin treasure. As you make your way through the hallways and chambers, monsters wield their conventional or spiritual weapons. You must gather the proper defenses along the way. But use them sparingly, the Minotaur looms closer!

That is all the lore there is. Reviews agree the story is minimal [F].

## Tarmin, the Minotaur and the treasure

- **Tarmin is a place, not a person.** It is "the island of Tarmin" on which the mazes were built, and the map screen is the "CASTLE MAP" of the "CASTLE TARMIN MAZE" [M, "Your Commands"]. Nothing in the manual or catalog names the Minotaur Tarmin; "his Tarmin treasure" [IL] is the treasure of the place.
- **The Minotaur is unnamed and is the villain.** "The DREADED MINOTAUR", "the DEVOURING MINOTAUR"; he and "his monster companions" built the mazes [M]. He is the only monster of one colour, purple, and "Guards the Tarmin treasure" [M, p. 29]. "To win the game you must slay the Minotaur and grab the Tarmin treasure!" [M, p. 29]. The game's working title was *Minotaur* [IL], and the 2014 Flashback and 2019 reprints retitled it *Minotaur* [F; BG].
- **The Treasure of Tarmin** is "the Great Treasure of dark ages", hidden with "other stolen riches" [M]: the treasure is plunder, not the Minotaur's own. It is drawn as a small yellow chest at the centre of the lowest level shown on the castle map [M, "Your Commands"].
- **The Minotaur sees the unseen.** A purple small potion makes you "invisible to all demons except the Minotaur" [M, p. 26]; invisibility objects work on "all monsters but the Minotaur" [M, "Know the Maze"]. Note the manual's one use of **"demons"** for the monsters.
- More Minotaurs appear on lower levels [W]; [M] says only that each level down may hold "the terrible Minotaur".

## War and Spiritual

Everything in the game has two sides, written "war/spiritual" with a slash [M, "Scoring"]:

- **Strength** is War/Spiritual: start maxima 49/24, raised by War books to 99/149/199 and Spiritual books to 49/74/99 [M, pp. 24-25].
- **Weapons** are War (bows, crossbows, knives, axes, darts, spears; colour = material: tan wood/leather, orange rusty iron, blue steel, gray silver, yellow gold, white platinum) or Spiritual (scrolls, books, small/large fireballs, small/large lightning bolts; colour = power: blue, gray, white, pink, red, purple) [M, pp. 16-17, 20-21].
- **Defence**: armour (shields, gauntlets, hauberks, helmets, breastplates) against war weapons, rings against spiritual weapons [M].
- **The maze itself is typed.** Eyeball murals mark maze entrances: dark green a war maze, blue a spiritual maze, tan a mixed maze. Gates in the hallways are green (more warlike), blue (more spiritual) or tan (no change) [M, "Know the Maze"].
- The monster's strength shows green for war, blue for spiritual [M, "Scoring"].

## The bestiary

The manual divides the monsters into three named classes, each "listed in order of increasing badness/nastiness" [M, pp. 27-29]. Colours run weakest to strongest. [Z] confirms each colour order from the sprites.

### Bad monsters: use spiritual weapons only

| Order | Monster | Colours |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Giant Ants | blue, pink, purple |
| 2 | Dwarfs | yellow, tan, orange |
| 3 | Giant Scorpions | blue, pink, purple |
| 4 | Dwarfs with shields | yellow, tan, orange |
| 5 | Giant Snakes | blue, pink, purple |
| 6 | Alligators | blue, pink, purple |
| 7 | Dragons | blue, pink, purple |

### Nasty monsters: use war weapons only

| Order | Monster | Colours (with and without shields) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Skeletons | white, gray, orange |
| 2 | Cloaked Skeletons | white, gray, orange |
| 3 | Giants | yellow, tan, orange |

### Horrible monsters: use either spiritual or war weapons

| Monster | Colours (with and without shields) |
|---|---|
| Ghouls ("short, hooded & robed") | white, gray, orange |
| Wraiths ("tall, hooded & robed") | white, gray, orange |
| Minotaur | purple only ("Guards the Tarmin treasure") |

"In harder two skill levels, horrible monsters will shatter your weakest of two scores" [M, p. 29].

**52 types in all** [M, p. 29]: 7 x 3 Bad (21) + 3 x 3 x 2 Nasty (18) + 2 x 3 x 2 Horrible (12) + the Minotaur (1) = 52. "You'll find more of the nasty and horrible ones in the deepest levels" [M].

Not in the manual's tables:
- **No Ghost.** There is no ghost in the original at all.
- **No crocodile:** the creature is the **Alligator**.
- **Doors with powers.** The manual warns of "OTHER DOORS...endowed with weird powers" and doors that behave strangely "because of an OMINOUS SPELL" [M]. Fan sources list an "Evil Door" monster (blue, tan, yellow) [Z]; [W] says door monsters drop the teleport, vision and Midas books. Treat the drops as unverified.
- **Bombs** in containers, three types, hurt war strength [M; Z].
- The manual's "Ghouls with shields" sprite may not exist in the cartridge [Z].

## Levels and difficulty

- Each level is a 10x10 centre maze inside outer corridors (12x12 with the ring) [M diagram; W]. Ladders go down only; from level 256 a ladder returns you to level 1 [M].
- The castle map shows up to 12 levels; the treasure sits in the middle of the lowest shown. "This is the first of 256 levels where the treasure can be found. Each level down is filled with nastier monsters and magic items...and maybe the terrible Minotaur!" [M].
- Four skill levels set the depth of the treasure (2, 4, 8 or 12 levels), the game's length (5-30 min up to 1.5-5 hr), vulnerability (1/4 to full), and the starting food and arrows [M, "Get Ready"].
- Container quality runs tan (mild), orange (good), blue (best) [M].

## Items of note

- Start: a bow, a quiver of arrows, a sack of flour, an empty pack (6 slots, 2 hands) [M].
- Treasures (coins, necklaces, ingots, lamps, chalices, crowns, each in silver, gold or platinum, from 10 to 600 points) [M, p. 24].
- Keys (tan, orange, blue), War books and Spiritual books, small and large potions, and the three **special books that never vanish**: blue Teleport, pink Vision, purple Midas ("turn treasures & war weapons platinum at your feet") [M, pp. 25-26].
- The tables are headed **"SECRETS OF THE UNIVERSE"** [M, p. 16].

## Gods, pantheon, the soul

- **No gods, no pantheon, no religion** appear in the manual or the catalog. The monsters are "monster companions", "ghastly occupants", "evil forces" and, once, "demons" [M; IL].
- **Reincarnation is canon in the original.** "If you DIE [...] However, cheer up! If you are worthy, you'll be REINCARNATED! And that means, a new opportunity for you to win!" [M, "Attacking!"]. In play the character returns elsewhere in the dungeon and loses most of their gear [BG]; a tombstone marks the death on the overview map [F]. This is the original's own seed of the Eternal Champion: a soul sent back while it is "worthy", stripped of its gear. Who judges worth is never said, which leaves room for the old gods.

## What this means for the myth (observations, not decisions)

1. **The original's three classes are a ready-made shape for a pantheon:** the seven **Bad** (spiritual) gods, the three **Nasty** (war) gods, and the two **Horrible** gods who wield both. The War/Spiritual split maps onto the new game's martial and Divinity sides.
2. **The original's Minotaur is the villain, not a god.** The current canon (CONTEXT.md, #267) already demotes the Minotaurs to Tarmin's generals and says Tarmin is not a Minotaur. The `EVENT_TARMIN_STATUE` ("Colossus of Minotaur Tarmin", "Minotaur King Tarmin") contradicts that canon twice: it names Tarmin a Minotaur and makes the enemy one of the old gods' shrines. It needs a decision: rename it to the Minotaur, drop it from the pantheon, or make it a defaced or captured shrine.
3. **"Tarmin" was a place before it was a name.** The island and castle of Tarmin give the new canon a faithful root for "Castle Tarmin". A myth in which the being took the island's name, or the island took his, would honour both.
4. **The treasure was stolen.** "The Great Treasure of dark ages", taken "along with other stolen riches" by the Minotaur's band. A myth in which the treasure was the old gods' own, taken when Tarmin's host overran them, fits the original's words.
5. **"If you are worthy, you'll be reincarnated"** is the original's own line. It is the closest thing the 1983 game has to the Eternal Champion and could be quoted in the new game.
6. **No Ghost and no Crocodile.** The Ghost and Old Dwarf shrines are inventions; the Crocodile is the original's Alligator.

## Caveats

- The manual OCR is noisy. The page images were checked for the story page, the weapon tables (pp. 16-17, 20-21) and every monster, treasure and item table (pp. 24-29); the rest (skill-level table, controls) is read from the OCR and may carry transcription errors.
- The archive.org scan is one printing (5300-0920). [F] lists a Swedish manual (*Tarmins Skattkammare*) and a typed German one, neither checked.
- No box-back text was found; the catalog text comes via Intellivision Lives, which reproduces Mattel's catalog but is not the catalog scan itself.
- Claims about gameplay not in the manual (more Minotaurs deeper, door-monster drops, losing gear on reincarnation) come from [W], [BG] and [Z], which are secondary and were not verified in an emulator.
